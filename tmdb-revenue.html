Python · Film revenue

Python · High-revenue films

Inflation-adjusted revenue vs budget, popularity, and genre. TMDb sample.

Analyst · Python · EDA

← Work EDA

About 10,000 TMDb titles. Dependent variable: inflation-adjusted revenue. The work is association, not a recipe for a hit.

The questions

  1. How do budget, popularity, vote average, and runtime relate to revenue_adj?
  2. Which genres tend to earn higher inflation-adjusted revenue?
  3. How have revenues and popularity moved by release year (1960–2015)?

Method

Pandas + seaborn on the TMDb movie CSV. Zeros in budget and revenue treated as missing (complete-case revenue work favors better-documented films). Multi-label genres expanded, so a film can sit in more than one bar. The numbers are the notebook’s.

Findings

Budget and popularity show the strongest positive associations with high inflation-adjusted revenue. Vote average and runtime are weaker. That is a pattern in this sample, not proof that buying a bigger budget causes a bigger box office.

Drama, Comedy, Thriller, and Action appear most often. Frequency is not the same as commercial performance. Among genres with at least 50 films that have known revenue:

Median revenue_adj in millions of 2010 USD. Animation n=237; Adventure n=843; Comedy n=1,771.

The extract includes far more recent movies (peak year 2014, 699 titles). Popularity is mildly higher for more recent films (year vs popularity corr 0.09). Mean inflation-adjusted revenue by year is volatile — blockbusters and missing financials move the line. Year vs revenue corr is −0.10.

So what

If you are ranking “what the data says about hits,” start with budget and popularity, then genre mix — not with “newer films automatically make more.” Comedy is everywhere and not the median-revenue leader.

Limitations

  • About half of movies have missing or zero budget/revenue.
  • Treating zeros as missing can hide a few true low-value titles.
  • Expanded genres double-count films.
  • TMDb popularity is platform-specific and may favor newer or more discussed titles.
  • No causal claim.

Next research I would actually run: ROI (revenue_adj / budget_adj) and genre performance inside the same decade, controlling for vote count.
