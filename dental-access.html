HRSA dental HPSA · Table 4 · 30 June 2026

A labeled analysis of HRSA’s dental Health Professional Shortage Areas. I did not work this as a client. I cleaned a public quarterly table so a hiring manager can see acquire → clean → visualize.

Public dataset. Source: HRSA Bureau of Health Workforce, Designated HPSA Quarterly Summary, Table 4, as of June 30, 2026 (data as of 1 July 2026; report dated 7 September 2026). data.hrsa.gov.

76.8MPeople in designated dental HPSAs
33.6%National percent of need met
12,760Dentists needed to lift designations

Problem

A dental ops analyst hears “we can’t hire.” The federal table already says where need is unmet. The PDF is 15 pages. Nobody ranks it in a huddle.

Approach

Extract Table 4, keep states and DC, leave territories in a note. Rank by dentists needed and by percent of need met. Chart the worst eight on each axis.

Insight

Need-met and headcount-needed are not the same list. Florida needs 1,314 dentists. Delaware meets 4.2% of need. Rural areas hold 64% of dental designations.

Impact

Recruiting, Medicaid, and mobile capacity should follow dentists-needed. Access programs should follow need-met. One number would send you to the wrong state.

Two rankings, not one

Percent of need met is dentists available ÷ dentists required to drop the area below the shortage threshold (generally 5,000:1 geographic, 4,000:1 high-need). Practitioners needed is the headcount gap. A small state can look catastrophic on the first metric and quiet on the second.

What I cleaned

  • Dropped region subtotals so they do not double-count states.
  • Kept the 50 states + DC in the table. Territories (PR, VI, Pacific) are in the national total but not in the rank bars — different scale and policy.
  • Did not invent county detail. The public extract is state grain.

Rural vs non-rural (Table 2): 5,093 of 7,951 dental designations are rural (64%). The people, though, skew more urban — non-rural designations cover 31.2M of the 76.8M. Shortage geography is rural; shortage headcount is often in cities.

Caveats a manager should hear

  • Facility HPSAs include some correctional populations. Automatic statutory sites are excluded from “practitioners needed” in the footnotes.
  • Need-met uses dentists only — not hygienists or mid-level providers.
  • This is a snapshot as of 30 June 2026, not a trend.
