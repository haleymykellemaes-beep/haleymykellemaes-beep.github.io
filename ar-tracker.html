At a Denver-area dental practice, the 90-day pile moved from 31.4% of open A/R to 18.7% — about a 40% reduction of the 90+ share. Those are the two dated Curve pulls I have. Excel A/R tracker for insurance vs patient aging so the morning can see which of the open dollars are insurance, which are patient, and how much is already 90 days old. Production rose in the same window and payer mix did not flip; I do not treat collections growth as something the workbook caused.

The question

I pull this from the live Curve Hero aging dashboard — not from a school dataset. Practice-management systems know the claim. The front office still needs a page that splits insurance A/R vs patient A/R, ages the open balance, and names the 90-day pile first.

Who do we work first, and is this money the payer’s or the patient’s?

Context, not a collections claim

Posted collections were higher in 2025 than 2024. Production went up; insurance stayed about 64% of collections both years and cards stayed in the low-thirties. I am not publishing the practice’s annual dollar mix by card type, and I am not saying the Excel A/R tracker caused the collections lift. The number I own is the 90+ share of open A/R.

What the Excel A/R tracker holds

Curve is the source of truth. The workbook is the morning follow-up list: placeholder IDs (RP-100x / P-20xx), not PMS IDs; aging buckets; a follow-up queue. Most recent visit and next appointment on the public file are demo dates so the morning follow-up list is visible without scheduling PHI. Dollar aging is from a 23 Sep 2026 90+ outstanding extract — labeled as 90+ only, not the full open board in the case study.

  • Ledger — Responsible Party Patient ID, Patient ID, visit dates, patient vs insurance aging. Some responsible parties cover 2–3 patients.
  • Calcs / Aging pivot — live SUMIFS: mix, 90+ share, accounts with no next appointment.
  • Aging pivot — live SUMIFS tables plus a pie: scheduled for next appointment vs not.

How a morning is supposed to run

  • Open the aging board. If 90+ is a third of open A/R, that is the first hour — not new production.
  • Insurance A/R means payer calls, ERAs, denials, appeals. Patient A/R means statements, plans, collections review.
  • Work the follow-up queue. When you touch a claim, set a new next-follow-up date so the count is honest tomorrow.
  • When money posts, enter the payment. Balance hits $0 and aging goes to —.

Limitations

Practice is unnamed here (a Denver-area dental practice). No patient names, no chart copies from the PMS, no provider names, no annual collections mix by card type.
