The question

I pull this from the live Curve Hero aging dashboard and the year-end collection summary — not from a school dataset. Practice-management systems know the claim. The front office still needs a page that splits insurance A/R vs patient A/R, ages the open balance, and names the 90-day pile first.

That mix is why the aging view has to split payer vs patient — most of the year’s cash came through insurance, but most of the stuck A/R is not.

What I still build in Excel

Curve is the source of truth. The workbook is the huddle layer: IDs not names, aging buckets, a follow-up queue. Most recent visit and next appointment on the public file are demo dates so the huddle workflow is visible without scheduling PHI. Dollar aging is from a 23 Sep 2026 outstanding extract.

  • Ledger — Responsible Party Patient ID, Patient ID, visit dates, patient vs insurance aging.
  • Calcs / Aging pivot — live SUMIFS: mix, 90+ share, accounts with no next appointment.
  • Aging pivot — live SUMIFS tables plus a pie: scheduled for next appointment vs not.

Download A/R tracker 110 accounts. IDs only. No names.

How a morning is supposed to run

  • Open the aging board. If 90+ is a third of open A/R, that is the first hour — not new production.
  • Insurance A/R means payer calls, ERAs, denials, appeals. Patient A/R means statements, plans, collections review.
  • Work the follow-up queue. When you touch a claim, set a new next-follow-up date so the count is honest tomorrow.
  • When money posts, enter the payment. Balance hits $0 and aging goes to —.
