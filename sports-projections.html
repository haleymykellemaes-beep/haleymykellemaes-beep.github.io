SRS · Super Bowl LIX

SRS · Projection vs the closing line

Projection PHI −3.5 vs close KC −1. Vegas hold 14.6%. Not a pick sheet.

A sportsbook analyst does not publish a “lock.” They post a number, take the other side of every ticket, and watch whether the close still matches the projection. When it does not, the question is hold and liability — not which team to back.

Portfolio model — not betting advice. No wagers, no bankroll, no “how to beat a book.” The artifact is the same table a trading desk would use: implied probability, de-vig, projection vs close, expected hold vs realized hold. I did not find a prior sports notebook of mine, so this is built from public 2024 NFL stats and published Super Bowl LIX lines.

The question

Did a transparent team-strength number disagree with the Super Bowl LIX close enough that a book should have treated the posted line as stale — and what did hold actually do once the game landed?

Regular-season NFL hold on a balanced −110 / −110 market is about four and a half cents. Super Bowls wander because the public is loud and the liabilities are not. Last year Vegas held 3.7% on Super Bowl LVIII. LIX it held 14.6%. That is a calibration and exposure story.

Method

  1. Projection. 2024 Simple Rating System from Pro-Football-Reference: Philadelphia 7.7, Kansas City 4.2. Difference 3.5 points toward the Eagles. Super Bowl is a neutral site, so I do not add home field. Call it PHI −3.5.
  2. Close. Consensus Super Bowl LIX close: Chiefs −1 to −1.5, moneyline about Chiefs −120 / Eagles +100 (opened near −120 / even; some books Eagles +105). Midpoint spread KC −1.25.
  3. Implied probability and hold. American odds → raw implied p. Sum minus 1 is the overround (posted hold if the book is balanced). Divide each side by the sum to de-vig.
  4. Spread → win probability. A desk rule of thumb: about 3 percentage points per point of spread. PHI −3.5 → 60.5% Eagles. I state the rule so a hiring manager can swap it.
  5. Compare to realized hold. Nevada Gaming Control Board handle and win, plus published ticket/money splits. Expected hold if balanced at −120 / +100 is 4.55%. Realized hold is a result, not a forecast.

American odds: negative p = |odds| / (|odds| + 100); positive p = 100 / (odds + 100). De-vig divides each raw p by 1.0455. This is a one-game board, not a 272-game backtest.

Posted hold

−120 / +100 sums to 104.55%. If the book is balanced, theoretical hold is 4.55% — a normal NFL moneyline. The close was not wild. The disagreement with SRS was.

Desk flag

Model 60.5% vs market 47.8% on Philadelphia is a double-digit probability gap. You do not “bet the Eagles.” You decide whether to shade the underdog, eat Chiefs tickets, or accept that the market is pricing dynasty more than point differential.

Public vs money

BetMGM (SI): Chiefs opened −1.5; 52% of tickets / 42% of money on Kansas City to cover. Eagles moneyline drew 63% of tickets / 60% of money. Tickets and dollars were not the same side on the spread.

Realized hold

Vegas: $151,618,159 handle, $22,134,104 win, 14.6% hold — vs $185.6M / 3.7% on LVIII. New York held 30.6% on $154.9M. Pennsylvania lost $6.5M on $101.6M (home-state Eagles). Hold is geography plus liability, not a posted vig.

So what

Two numbers a morning meeting can argue with:

  1. The close was ~4.8 points away from a dumb, public SRS. Kansas City won more games. Philadelphia outscored people by 160 to 59. The market faded the point differential. After the fact the Eagles won by 18 — that does not prove the model, but it is the direction the exception report was pointing.
  2. Expected hold was 4.6%. Vegas realized 14.6%. That is almost four times Super Bowl LVIII. A book that was long Eagles (or short the public favorite) had a historic Sunday. A book that was stuck on Philadelphia — Pennsylvania — paid it back. The EV table is for exposure, not for a parlay card.

What I would ship in a shop: a nightly file of closing line vs model, a residual in points and in probability, and a hold waterfall (posted vig → expected hold given ticket mix → realized). Flag games with a >1-point close/model gap and a ticket/money split. LIX would have lit up both.

Limitations

  • One Super Bowl, not a season-long calibration. I will not pretend a Brier score from n = 1.
  • SRS is last year’s regular season. It ignores playoff injuries, rest, and Super Bowl-specific game plans.
  • The 3%-per-point conversion is a rule of thumb, not a fitted logistic. A different mapping moves 60.5%.
  • Closing lines varied by book (−1 vs −1.5, +100 vs +105). I used the published consensus band, not a single sportsbook dump.
  • State hold includes futures, props, and parlays, not only the moneyline in the table. Vegas 14.6% is the whole Super Bowl book.
  • Not betting advice. A portfolio model of public stats. Do not use it to place a wager.
