Public 10-K

Replenish the growth. Cut the slow pile.

Premium athletic analog · FY2024 10-K · inventory scenario

Analyst · 10-K analog · inventory

← Work Forecast · not an employer

A data analyst at a premium athletic retailer does not need another scatterplot. They need a buy: which region and category to replenish before the next season, and which core depth to cut so holiday leftovers do not become an outlet problem.

Public-data analog — I did not work at lululemon. No client file. I used the company’s FY2024 results (year ended 2 Feb 2025) the way a DA would: mix, comps, inventory, then a replenish-vs-cut call. A July 2026 management class deck of mine already pointed at Americas concentration, men’s and international as the growth bet, and protecting a ~58% gross margin. This page is the numbers layer on that same 10-K, not a job claim.

The question

If next year’s revenue is guided +5% to +7%, should the next inventory buy copy last year’s +9% unit build — or follow sell-through?

The 10-K itself names the risk: inventory above demand becomes write-downs and discounted product, “which would cause our gross margin to suffer and could impair the strength and exclusivity of our brand.” Outlets exist for that leftover (52 locations, mostly Americas). The DA job is to keep the leftover off the buy sheet.

Method

  1. Take the public mix. Region, channel, category, store count, inventory, COGS — FY2024 vs FY2023 from the 8-K / 10-K, not a scraped store file.
  2. Separate growth from productivity. Total Americas +4% can hide comps −1%. Stores +14% revenue can hide sales per square foot $1,574 vs $1,609.
  3. Turn inventory into a rate. $1.442B inventory on $4.317B COGS is 2.99 turns, about 122 days on hand (121 days in FY2023). Turns did not save you. Mix did not improve.
  4. Project FY2025 two ways. Naive: inventory +9% again. Mix-aware: grow the buy where comps and category are still running, hold the 75% Americas core.
  5. Call the buy. Replenish / hold / cut — the decision, not a chart of everything that went up.

Two FY2025 inventory scenarios

Company guide on 27 Mar 2025: FY2025 net revenue $11.150B–$11.300B (+5% to +7%, or +7% to +8% excluding the extra week in 2024). Midpoint $11.225B, about +6%.

Naive replenish

Copy last year’s inventory build: +9% → $1.57B sitting. Americas is still ~75% of sales and comps just fell. Q4 is 34% of revenue and ~42% of operating profit — leftover after holiday is the markdown window. Extra units in the slow region become outlet or a 59.2% margin problem.

Mix-aware buy

Grow inventory with the +6% guide globally (~$1.53B), but do not spread it evenly. Hold or cut Americas women’s core depth. Put the incremental units in China Mainland / international doors (151 China stores, up from 127) and men’s (+14%).

If Americas only repeats its FY2024 ex-53rd pace (+2%), it lands near $8.09B. The $11.225B midpoint then needs international at about $3.14B (+18% off $2.66B) — even if China cools from +41%. That is the mix the buy has to fund. A +9% Americas restock does not.

Proxy math, not a SKU file: 74.9% of $1.442B is ~$1.08B of Americas-side inventory. Another +9% there is ~$97M more units into a −1% comp market. The gap between a +9% global build and a +6% guide-matched build is ~$43M. Either number is a markdown conversation, not a rounding error.

The decision (analog families)

No guest waitlist and no SKU sell-through was published. I will not invent one. The public proxies are good enough to rank the buy:

Analog family Public proxy Call
International hero China Mainland +41%, comps +25%; intl comps +22% Replenish — doors and units
Men’s training Men’s $2.558B, +14% (+12% ex-53rd); still 24% of sales Replenish — depth is not the 63% women’s pile
Americas women’s core Women’s 63% of $; Americas comps −1%; 52 outlets already exist Hold / cut depth — protect 59.2% GM
Americas e-comm E-comm +4% ex-53rd; one inventory pool feeds stores and web Hold — do not double-buy the same unit

So what

Total revenue +10% is the wrong control tower. Americas comps −1% with inventory +9% is the exception report. Replenish China and men’s. Cut core Americas depth before Q4 leftover hits the 52 outlets. That is how you defend 59.2% gross margin without a cost-leadership race — the same differentiation stance as the management read of this 10-K, turned into a buy sheet.

FY2025 guide is only +5% to +7%. A buy that assumes last year’s +9% inventory and last year’s China +41% will overbuy the slow side and underbuy the side that still has to carry the $12.5B Power of Three ×2 plan (from $6.25B in 2021).

Limitations

  • Company totals, not SKU, size, or store-cluster sell-through. A real merchant file could reverse a family-level call.
  • Inventory is not published by region. The $1.08B Americas proxy assumes inventory follows revenue mix.
  • FY2024 had a 53rd week. I used the company’s ex-53rd comps and growth where they published them.
  • Guide is as of 27 Mar 2025. Macro, tariffs, and later quarters can move it.
  • This is a portfolio analog, not advice to lululemon or a claim I sat in their planning meeting.

What I would run with an actual export: SKU-level sell-through and waitlist conversion by region, weeks-of-supply vs a 122-day company average, and a markdown-risk flag on Americas women’s core that is still deep after week 8.
